ⓘ approach

Every star feels one fixed “field”: the galaxy's gravitational potential, built from smooth components (thin + thick disk, bulge, halo) plus two rigidly rotating m=2 waves — a bar and a logarithmic spiral. Stars are integrated on their own orbits with a symplectic leapfrog; there are no star–star forces and no friction.

The spiral arms are not material — they are the rotating crests of the field where stars bunch up as they pass, so the pattern never winds up while each star streams through it. The bar works the same way: inside corotation its field captures stars onto elongated resonant (x1) orbits, feeding the bar; at resonances stars trade angular momentum with the pattern and migrate — outward into rings, or onto eccentric inclined orbits that puff into the halo belt. That is how disk turns into bar and belt without spawning anything.

Because the system is Hamiltonian — dissipation free, symplectically integrated — nothing decays, nothing clumps; the same stars are there forever. (Optional “drag” toggle shows the opposite: pure friction piles stars into the center — why friction is the wrong tool here.)
