# 0.1.0 Plan — Galaxy star-movement demo (fixed rotating field, no N-body)

Source draft: `draft.md` (2026-09-22).

## 1. Purpose

Single-HTML/JS canvas demo, 1k stars default / 10k target, 60 fps, `file://`-friendly,
no build. Every star is a persistent observable object on its own orbit. A spiral galaxy's
features — thin+thick disk, bulge, bar, spiral arms, halo belt — emerge as families of orbits
in one shared, rigidly rotating gravitational field. Nothing is a rigid group: stars stream
through bar and arms, trade angular momentum at resonances, and migrate between disk, bar and
belt by real (collisionless) dynamics. The demo must be numerically stable for arbitrarily
long runs and carry a short in-page explanation of the approach.

## 2. Non-goals

- Self-consistent self-gravity (N-body, PM/tree, Poisson solve) — explicitly excluded by draft; also O(N²) or heavy, and not needed for stable patterns.
- Gas, hydro, star formation/evolution, transient arm generation (swing amplification), colliding-particle "granular" friction.
- Spawn/despawn particle systems (see Decision 3).
- Mobile UI, accessibility polish, i18n.

## 3. Key decisions (answers to the draft's questions)

| # | Option | Verdict | Why |
|---|--------|---------|-----|
| 1 | N-body gravity | rejected | Draft asks for fast math without it; 10²  –10³ times cheaper without; two-body relaxation is irrelevant to the effect we show. |
| 2 | Velocity-proportional drag as "cheap gravity" to feed belt/bar | rejected as default; kept as a teaching toggle | Drag is dissipative: it circularizes and shrinks orbits, stars pile up near the potential minimum — the draft's suspicion ("not stable, ends with all stars in some locations") is correct. Real structural transport is collisionless: resonant angular-momentum exchange with the rotating non-axisymmetric field does it without any friction. Optional toggles `drag` (shows the collapse) and `drag+diffusion` Langevin (σ² = 2η⟨v²⟩, restores a stationary state) demonstrate both failure and fix. |
| 3 | Spawn/despawn visual particles encoding density | rejected | Draft prefers stable observable stars; a density-wave field already gives time-independent patterns from a fixed population, so spawning adds nothing physical. |
| 4 | Closed-form epicycle superposition (analytic rosettes, no integration) | rejected | Cheapest and unconditionally stable, but closed forms cannot carry non-axisymmetric coupling: no bar capture, no resonances, no migration — the heart of the draft's question. |
| 5 | Chosen: leapfrog integration of test stars in a fixed multi-component analytic potential; bar + spiral as rigidly rotating m=2 Fourier modes (Lin–Shen density waves) | adopted | This *is* the "predefined galaxy field" option, made stable by construction: Hamiltonian, no dissipation, symplectic integrator → no clumping, bounded energy/Jacobi drift, stars observable through all history. |

Consequence for spiral arms: arms are a **pattern rotating at a constant pattern speed Ωs**,
not material. Differential rotation destroys any material arm within ~2 rotations (winding
problem); a rigid pattern never dies, and stars visibly overtake / fall behind the arms —
"realistic orbits", not rigid groups. The bar field rotates at its own Ωb > Ωs ("field rotates
with the galaxy" from the draft).

## 4. Physics model

Units: G = 1, v0 = 1 (flat rotation speed at R0), R0 = 1. Time in units of Ω(R0)⁻¹; the HUD
shows rotations. All potentials below have closed-form gradients; force = −∇Φ summed over
components.

Backbone (axisymmetric, static):

- Thin disk + thick disk (scale length/b) — Miyamoto–Nagase:
  `Φ = −G Md / sqrt(R² + (a + sqrt(b² + z²))²)`, S = sqrt(b²+z²), D = sqrt(R²+(a+S)²),
  `fR = −G Md R / D³`, `fz = −G Md (a+S) z / (S D³)`.
- Bulge — Plummer: `f = −G Mb r / (r² + s²)^{3/2}`.
- Halo (hosts the belt) — softened log: `Φ = ½ vh² ln(r² + rc²)`, `f = −vh² r / (r² + rc²)`.

Rotating non-axisymmetric modes (each m=2, rigid pattern speed):

- Bar: `Φb = Ab fb(r) cos(2(φ − Ωb t)) exp(−z²/hb²)`, with `fb(r) = (r/rb)² / (1 + (r/rb)⁴)`
  → inside the bar the potential is ≈ harmonic with a rotating elliptical contour, so x1-type
  elongated orbits exist analytically; beyond rb it decays fast so the outer disk stays clean.
- Spiral: `Φs = As fs(r) sech²(z/zs) cos(2(φ − Ωs t − p ln(r/r1)))` — logarithmic spiral density
  wave; `fs(r)` ramps from 0 at r < 0.6 to a plateau by r ≈ 2 (cap the ln r winding: freeze
  phase at r1 below the ramp), pitch angle ≈ 12–20° (p = tan pitch). As ≈ 0.015–0.03 v0² for a
  visible but non-disruptive wave.
- Optional upgrade (post-0.1): Cox & Wainwright 2004 triaxial bar instead of the Fourier bar.

Tuning target: `vc(r)` within 0.7–1.2 across r ∈ [0.3, 5] (near-flat), κ² > 0 everywhere,
κ/Ω ≈ √2 at R0. Resonance radii computed numerically from Ω(r), κ(r) and drawn as rings:
ILR `Ω − κ/2 = Ωb`, CR `Ω = Ωb`, OLR `Ω + κ/2 = Ωb`, analogously for the spiral.

Transport narrative (what the draft asks "how do stars get to belt and bar"):

1. Inside corotation the bar's field exerts a persistent torque; stars are captured onto x1
   orbits aligned with the bar → the bar is *made of* disk stars on resonant orbits, and
   "disk→bar" happens by capture, not spawning.
2. At/near corotation stars exchange angular momentum and shift guiding radius without heating
   (radial migration / churning) → disk stars diffuse outward, seeding rings and belt populations.
3. Stars scattering through the bar's ILR gain eccentricity and vertical energy (vertical ILR
   produces the box/peanut → "inner belt"/thick-component growth) → high-e, inclined halo-like
   orbits. All three are emergent; nothing is forced per star.

## 5. Stars and initial conditions

State of array, `Float64Array` per field: `x y z vx vy vz`, `Uint8Array class`. Allocate for
Nmax = 10 000 once; running N is a prefix (toggle 1k/10k = copy class/params prefix). No
object literals per star, ever (AGENTS hot-path rule).

Populations, each sampled from its own DF (analytic, no relaxation needed for stability since
the field is fixed):

| class | share | sampling |
|-------|-------|----------|
| thin disk | 55% | Σ ∝ exp(−R), h = 1; R from inverse CDF `R = −W(−0.368(1−u))−1` (Lambert-W or tabled approx of exponential-disk CDF); φ uniform; `vφ = vc(R) + 0.10 ξ1`, `vR = 0.07 ξ2`, `z = 0.05 ξ3`, `vz = 0.05 ξ4` |
| thick disk | 25% | h = 1.6, σφ 0.2 v0, flared scale height `0.2·R` |
| bulge | 10% | Plummer radii by inverse CDF, isotropic 0.6 v0 |
| halo belt | 8% | 400 isotropic eccentric orbits r ∈ [1, 6] |
| belt stream | 2% | 200 stars phase-locked within ~2% in E and ±0.03 in Lz around one chosen eccentric inclined orbit → visible stream/belt wrapping the halo; it will phase-mix (stretch, gaps) exactly like real streams while remaining stable |

ξ = standard normal (Box–Muller, seeded xorshift128+ PRNG for full determinism). After init,
integrate 20 time units as a settle (structures equilibrate before observation begins); reset
replays the same seed.

## 6. Integration & stability

- Leapfrog (kick–drift–kick), fixed `dt = 0.01`; substeps per frame = `round(timeScale/dt)`,
  timeScale slider 0.02–0.2, default 0.1 (10 substeps).
- Time-dependent potential: leapfrog keeps Jacobi-type E bounded (no secular damping); gate on
  drift (tests §8).
- Hot path: no allocations, no destructuring/closures in loops (AGENTS). Polar/trig budget:
  cos2φ = (x²−y²)/r², sin2φ = 2xy/r² computed algebraically (no atan2); per star per mode only
  one `Math.log(r)` + one sin/cos pair for the time-phase (computed as
  `cos(2ψ)=cos2φ·cosβ−sin2φ·sinβ`, β = 2Ωp t + 2p·ln r). Soften: clamp r < 0.02; speed cap
  3·v0 (belt against slingshot numerical escape, never expected to engage).
- Perf budget: sim ≤ 8 ms, render ≤ 6 ms at N=10k. 10k × 10 substeps × ~50 flops + ~250k
  transcendental calls/frame is comfortable for V8; if not, first lever is timeScale, then a
  per-star "far from pattern" cheap-path (skip modes where fs(r) ≈ 0).

## 7. Rendering, views, controls (all in index.html)

- Canvas 2D, DPR-aware. Projection: near-face-on orthographic with tilt τ≈20°:
  `sx = x`, `sy = y·cos τ − z·sin τ`; slight depth cue: radius and alpha scale by (1 − 0.25·|z|/3).
- Sprites: one pre-rendered 16×16 glow bitmap per class into offscreen canvases at init;
  per frame `globalCompositeOperation='lighter'` + `drawImage` (no gradients/fills per star).
  Trails option: paint fade rect `rgba(0,0,0,α)` instead of clearing.
- Colors: thin = warm white, thick = pale orange, bulge = orange-red, halo/belt = blue-white,
  stream = bright cyan, bar-captured tracers = green.
- Overlays (checkboxes):
  - resonance rings + labels (ILR/CR/OLR for bar and spiral);
  - rotation curve mini-plot: theory `vc(r)`, measured mean `vφ(r)` histogram, and Ωp lines;
  - orbit trails for 8 highlighted stars (ring buffers 240 pts) — the "realistic orbits" proof:
    rosettes, x1 boxy/x2 crosses, stream stretching;
  - frame toggle: inertial ↔ corotating with bar (`ctx.rotate(−Ωb t)` on draw only — cheap and
    shows quasi-steady field with stars moving through it: the draft's "field rotates with galaxy").
- HUD: N, fps, time in rotations, max |ΔE_J|/E_J since reset, legend, "ⓘ approach" text (§Appendix A).
- Controls: N 1k/10k; sliders: timeScale, Ab, As, drag η, diffusion σ; checkboxes: bar, spiral,
  belt stream, drag, drag+diff, trails, rings, rotcurve, corot; pause/step; reset (same seed).
  Keys: space pause, s step, r reset, c corot, [1..5] toggle overlays.
- UI code lives inside index.html; physics never touches DOM.

## 8. Tests & metrics

`experiments/` node scripts, `require('../galaxy.js')` (module.exports-guarded), deterministic seed:

- `check_gradient`: analytic ∇Φ vs central difference on a 3D grid, all components and both modes; rel. err < 1e-4.
- `check_rotation_curve`: measured vc(r) vs target; |dev| < 10% for r ∈ [0.3, 5]; κ² > 0; κ/Ω ∈ [1.35, 1.5] near R0.
- `check_epi_ratio`: single star radial/azimuthal period ratio matches 2Ω/κ within 2%.
- `check_jacobi`: N=1000, T=200 (with bar+spiral on): max |ΔE_J|/|E_J| < 1e-3 (per pattern: use E−ΩpLz with the dominant term).
- `check_no_clumping`: radial Σ(r) and vertical |z| profiles at T=0 vs T=100: relative L2 < 5%; peak/mean density ratio stays below its settled value + 20% (drag-ON run is expected to fail this — that is the demonstration).
- `check_resonances`: after T=100 with bar on, density ring/gap maxima within 0.1 of predicted ILR/CR radii.
- `check_drag_collapse` (opt-in documentation test): with pure drag, median r drops >30% by T=50.

Browser smoke checklist (manual) at each milestone: file:// open, 60 fps @ default, toggles no-op-safe, resize OK.

## 11. Risks & mitigations

- 10k drawImage cost — sprites are 16×16 prerendered; fallback path: additive ImageData
  splatting; last resort: default N=4k with 10k opt-in (still meets spec since 10k "or").
- V8 trig budget — accounted in §6; if still slow, precompute `fs`, ramps at 256-entry table.
- Time-dependent potential + leapfrog resonance noise at small r — r clamp + speed cap; if
  outliers appear, cap As at large time via soft global ramp during first 10 t.u.
- Axisymmetric DF in a bar field → initial transient — settle phase + moderate Ab (start 0.05,
  slider to 0.15).
- "Belt" terminology — assumed thick disk/halo + streams; if the user meant rings/belts as
  overtone rings (ansae at OLR), the resonance-ring machinery already renders them (M3).
- Jacobi drift alarm — integrator quality is the single point of failure for "stable through
  all history"; §8 gates it numerically at every milestone, not just visually.

## Appendix A — in-page description (final wording for the demo)

> Every star here feels one fixed "field": the galaxy's gravitational potential, built from
> smooth components (thin + thick disk, bulge, halo) plus two rigidly rotating m=2 waves —
> a bar and a logarithmic spiral. Stars are integrated on their own orbits with a symplectic
> leapfrog; there are no star–star forces and no friction. The spiral arms are not material —
> they are the rotating crests of the field where stars bunch up and slow down as they pass, so
> the pattern never winds up while each star streams through it. The bar works the same way:
> inside corotation its field captures stars onto elongated resonant (x1) orbits, feeding the
> bar; at resonances stars trade angular momentum with the pattern and migrate — outward into
> rings, or onto eccentric inclined orbits that puff into the halo belt. That is how disk turns
> into bar and belt without spawning anything. Because the system is Hamiltonian — dissipation
> free, symplectically integrated — nothing decays, nothing clumps; the same stars are there
> forever. (Optional "drag" toggle shows the opposite: pure friction piles stars into the
> center — why friction is the wrong tool here.)

## Appendix B — formula crib

- v_c²(R) = Σ_i R·(−∂Φi/∂R); Ω = vc/R; κ² = R·dΩ²/dR + 4Ω²; ν² = (1/R³)·∂z²Φ|z=0 scaled.
- MN: above in §4. Plummer: Φ = −GM/√(r²+s²). Log: Φ = ½vh² ln(r²+rc²).
- Mode gradient from Φ = A f(r) cos(mψ), ψ = φ − Ωp t − α ln r:
  `fφ = m A f sin(mψ)` (tangential force), `fr = −A (f' cos(mψ) + m f α / r · (−sin(mψ)))` signs
  per derivation in code, verified by `check_gradient`.
- Jacobi: E_J = ½v² + Φ − Ωp Lz per pattern (dominant pattern used for the gate).
- Exponential-disk radius draw: solve r+… by tabled inverse CDF (build a 1024-entry lookup at
  init; no per-star Newton).
